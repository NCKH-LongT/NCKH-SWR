## Week 5 Questions

### Question 1: Where and how has this topic been studied?

- Academic procrastination and AI-driven learning assistants have been widely studied in higher education worldwide.
- Existing research mainly focuses on individual aspects, including:
  - Machine Learning models for predicting academic procrastination.
  - Large Language Model (LLM)-based conversational agents for learning support.
  - Time management techniques such as Pomodoro and intelligent scheduling.
  - Requirements Engineering (RE) methods for AI-enabled software systems.
- Most studies evaluate only a single capability rather than an integrated AI solution.

### Question 2: What research gaps remain?

- There is no comprehensive AI-based study assistant that integrates adaptive scheduling, procrastination prediction, conversational coaching, explainable AI, and personalized intervention.
- Requirements Engineering processes for AI educational assistants remain incomplete, particularly regarding stakeholder elicitation, traceability, and validation.
- Existing systems provide limited explainability and transparency, reducing users' trust in AI-generated recommendations.
- Longitudinal validation in real university environments, especially within Vietnam and Southeast Asia, is still lacking.

### Question 3: What practical problem does the team aim to clarify?

- Students are increasingly distracted by short-form social media platforms, leading to chronic academic procrastination, poor time management, stress, and declining academic performance.
- Conventional productivity tools such as static to-do lists and basic Pomodoro timers are insufficient because they lack personalization and behavioral adaptation.
- The project aims to investigate how an AI-powered personal assistant can:
  - Understand students' learning behaviors and procrastination patterns.
  - Predict procrastination risk.
  - Deliver personalized reminders and conversational support.
  - Encourage sustainable study habits through adaptive interventions.

### Question 4: What research questions will the team investigate?

**Main Research Question**

- How can requirements be specified and validated for an AI-driven study assistant that helps students manage tasks, reminders, study schedules, and academic procrastination?

**Sub Research Questions**

- RQ1: What requirements should be elicited from students to understand their study planning habits, procrastination behaviors, and reminder preferences?
- RQ2: What functional and non-functional requirements are required for task management, intelligent reminders, focus monitoring, motivational feedback, and AI-based conversational support?
- RQ3: How can a prototype be used to validate the usefulness, usability, and effectiveness of AI-generated reminders, task planning, and progress tracking?
- RQ4: How can explainability, transparency, and trustworthy AI requirements be specified so that students understand and trust the recommendations generated by the system?

# Problem Statement

Academic procrastination has become one of the most significant challenges affecting students' learning performance in the digital era. Continuous exposure to short-form social media platforms such as TikTok, Facebook Reels, and YouTube Shorts encourages instant gratification, shortens attention spans, and increases the likelihood of chronic procrastination. As a result, many students postpone academic tasks until deadlines approach, leading to increased stress, poor learning outcomes, and reduced time-management skills.

Although numerous studies have explored machine learning models for procrastination prediction, intelligent tutoring systems, conversational AI, and time-management applications, these solutions typically address only isolated aspects of the problem. Existing systems rarely integrate behavioral prediction, adaptive scheduling, personalized conversational coaching, gamification, and explainable AI into a single end-to-end solution.

Furthermore, the literature reveals several important research gaps. Current Requirements Engineering approaches are insufficient for AI-driven educational assistants because they lack systematic stakeholder requirement elicitation, AI-specific validation processes, and traceability between user needs and AI functionality. In addition, existing solutions provide limited explainability and transparency, making it difficult for students to understand or trust AI-generated recommendations. Most empirical evaluations are also conducted using small datasets, short experimental periods, or single-country case studies, limiting their applicability to real-world university environments, particularly in Vietnam and Southeast Asia.

Therefore, this research proposes an AI-driven personal assistant designed to mitigate academic procrastination and improve students' time-management skills. The proposed system integrates behavioral analytics, adaptive study scheduling, procrastination risk prediction, LLM-based conversational support, and personalized interventions within a unified architecture. The primary objective is to specify and validate the system requirements while evaluating its effectiveness, usability, and trustworthiness in supporting Vietnamese university students.
