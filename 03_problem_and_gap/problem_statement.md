## Problem Statement

In higher education, particularly in software engineering and IT courses with large student cohorts, instructors face persistent challenges in creating CLO-aligned assessments and delivering timely, personalized feedback. These tasks are time-consuming when done manually, and existing Learning Management Systems provide little support for automating them in a way that respects curriculum structure. In the Vietnamese higher education context specifically, LMS adoption remains limited by the mismatch between what these systems offer and what instructors actually need in their day-to-day workflows.

While RAG-based LMS systems have emerged as a technically promising solution, the research community has focused almost exclusively on building and evaluating the AI components of these systems — measuring faithfulness, answer relevance, and generation quality — without first establishing what stakeholders require from them. No prior work has formally specified the functional and non-functional requirements for a RAG-based quiz generation system from the perspective of instructors and students, nor has any work defined how those requirements should be validated. This gap means that technically capable systems are being built without a clear understanding of whether they actually meet real educational needs, making reliable design, adoption, and evaluation of such systems difficult in practice.
