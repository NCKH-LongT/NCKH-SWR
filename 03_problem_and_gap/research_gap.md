## Gap 

Current studies address isolated aspects of academic procrastination (prediction, scheduling, chatbots, self-regulated learning, or Requirements Engineering), but **no existing work proposes and validates a comprehensive AI-based personal assistant that integrates Requirements Engineering, adaptive scheduling, early procrastination prediction, LLM-based conversational intervention, explainable AI, and real-world evaluation in a university context.** Additionally, most studies are validated on limited populations and lack systematic requirements elicitation from students, long-term deployment, and evaluation in Southeast Asian educational settings.

---


### Gap 1 – Lack of an integrated end-to-end AI solution

- **Derived from:** Papers 2, 3, 4, 5.
- Existing studies focus on only one capability (planning, prediction, or chatbot intervention). None combines adaptive scheduling, ML prediction, behavioral intervention, and LLM personalization into one architecture.
- **The gap:** An integrated AI assistant supporting the complete procrastination management lifecycle is still missing.

### Gap 2 – Requirements Engineering for AI assistants remains insufficient

- **Derived from:** Papers 1, 2, 4.
- RE studies are generic while education-oriented systems rarely perform systematic requirements elicitation or traceability.
- **The gap:** There is no complete RE framework for AI academic assistants that links stakeholder needs, AI functions, and validation.

### Gap 3 – Limited explainability, transparency, and trustworthy AI

- **Derived from:** Papers 1, 4, 5.
- Existing AI systems provide recommendations but rarely explain why recommendations are generated or define measurable explainability criteria.
- **The gap:** Technical standards for explainability, trust, and AI recommendation transparency remain missing.

### Gap 4 – Limited real-world validation and contextual generalization

- **Derived from:** Papers 2, 3, 4, 5.
- Most studies use small samples, short intervention periods, or single-country datasets.
- **The gap:** Long-term validation across different educational contexts, especially Southeast Asia and Vietnam, has not been conducted.
