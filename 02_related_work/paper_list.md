# Selected Papers for Literature Review

| Paper Title | Research Method | Reason for Selection | Relevant RQ(s) | Emerging Theme |
|------------|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| **Preparing Business Analysts for the Modern Job Market: A Comparative Analysis of Skills and Education** (Agnieszka Kowalska-Styczeń, Kinga Juszczyk) | Comparative analysis of BA education and labor market requirements | Directly examines Business Analyst competencies and compares academic preparation with industry expectations. | RQ1, RQ2, RQ3 | Traditional BA Skills; Academia–Industry Gap |
| **Recommendations for Efficient and Responsible LLM Adoption within Industrial Software Development** (Krishna Ronanki, Beatriz Cabrero-Daniel, Tomas Herda, Stefan Sitkovich, Jennifer Horkoff, Christian Berger) | Industrial study and practice recommendations | Provides practical guidance on AI adoption, including prompt engineering, responsible AI use, and domain knowledge. | RQ1, RQ3 | AI Literacy; AI Literacy Gap |
| **The Changing Nature of Product Management Work: Where Large Language Models Fit and Where They Don’t** (Pietari Immonen) | Qualitative study based on interviews with industry practitioners | Explores how AI changes knowledge-intensive work and highlights the need to combine domain expertise with AI capabilities. | RQ1, RQ3 | Dual Literacy; Human–AI Collaboration |
| **Decoding the AI Job Market: Mapping Skills and Classifying Careers Through Online Job Ads** (Maayan Nakash, Or Peretz) | Analysis of 18,947 LinkedIn AI-related job postings | Provides empirical evidence of AI-related skill demand in the labor market, including business-oriented roles. | RQ2, RQ3 | Hybrid Skills; Business–Technology Bridging Role |
| **Generative AI for Requirements Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review** (Haowei Cheng et al.) | Systematic Literature Review (238 studies) | Provides a comprehensive overview of GenAI applications in Requirements Engineering and identifies the gap between research and industrial adoption. | RQ1, RQ3 | AI-Enhanced Requirements Engineering Skills; Academia–Industry Gap |
| **A Systematic Mapping of Large Language Models for Improving Natural Language Software Requirements** (Raiane Eunice C. Santos Fernandes, Fabio Gomes Rocha, Rheidner Achiles Couto S. Fernandes) | Systematic Mapping Study (12 studies) | Identifies emerging competencies related to LLM-assisted Requirements Engineering activities. | RQ1, RQ3 | AI-Enhanced Requirements Engineering Skills |
| **The Role of Generative AI Models in Requirements Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review** (Poonkuzhali Vasudevan, Sandeep Reddivari) | Systematic Literature Review (44 studies) | Reviews the use of Generative AI across Requirements Engineering activities and discusses current evidence and limitations. | RQ1, RQ3 | AI-Enhanced Requirements Engineering Skills |
| **Large Language Model for Requirements Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review** (Javed Ali Khan, Shamaila Qayyum, Hafsa Shareef Dar) | Systematic Literature Review | Synthesizes current applications of LLMs in Requirements Engineering and highlights AI-supported RE practices. | RQ1, RQ3 | AI-Enhanced Requirements Engineering Skills |
| **Applying Large Language Models to Requirements Engineering: Opportunities and Challenges in Traceability, Quality Review and Requirements Extraction** (Marielle Gustafsson) | Literature review and industrial case study | Provides real-world evidence of LLM adoption in Requirements Engineering and highlights practical challenges and opportunities. | RQ1, RQ3 | Human–AI Collaboration; AI-Enhanced Requirements Engineering Skills |

# Cross-RQ Theme Analysis

| Theme | Description | Supporting Papers |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Traditional BA Skills** | Core Business Analyst competencies remain important despite AI adoption. | Kowalska-Styczeń & Juszczyk |
| **AI Literacy** | Understanding LLMs, prompt engineering, AI evaluation, and responsible AI use is increasingly required. | Ronanki et al.; Immonen |
| **Dual Literacy** | Professionals need both domain expertise and AI interaction capabilities. | Immonen |
| **Hybrid Skills** | AI-related roles increasingly require a combination of technical and soft skills. | Nakash & Peretz; Ronanki et al. |
| **Human–AI Collaboration** | AI augments human work rather than replacing human judgment and decision-making. | Immonen; Gustafsson |
| **AI-Enhanced Requirements Engineering Skills** | LLMs are increasingly applied to requirements elicitation, analysis, validation, traceability, and documentation. | Cheng et al.; Fernandes et al.; Vasudevan & Reddivari; Khan et al.; Gustafsson |
| **Business–Technology Bridging Role** | Professionals increasingly connect business objectives with AI-enabled solutions. | Nakash & Peretz |
| **Academia–Industry Gap** | Academic research and educational preparation lag behind evolving industry requirements. | Kowalska-Styczeń & Juszczyk; Cheng et al. |
