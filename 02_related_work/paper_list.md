

# RQ1. What skills are identified in the existing literature as essential for Business Analysts working in software development environments where AI tools are being adopted?

| Paper Title | Methodology / Data Source | Key Insights & Reason for Selection | Contribution to RQ / Emerging Theme |
|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| **Preparing Business Analysts for the Modern Job Market: A Comparative Analysis of Skills and Education** (Agnieszka Kowalska-Styczeń, Kinga Juszczyk) | Comparative analysis of BA education and labor market requirements | Identifies core BA competencies including analytical thinking, communication, technical literacy, teamwork, adaptability, and project management. Highlights increasing demand for data-related and digital technology skills. | **Primary evidence** for foundational BA competencies. <br> **Theme:** Traditional BA Skills |
| **Recommendations for Efficient and Responsible LLM Adoption within Industrial Software Development** (Krishna Ronanki et al.) | Industrial software development context; recommendations derived from LLM adoption experiences | Highlights the importance of domain knowledge, understanding LLM capabilities and limitations, prompt engineering, and responsible AI use. | **Primary evidence** for AI-related competencies. <br> **Theme:** AI Literacy |
| **The Changing Nature of Product Management Work: Where Large Language Models Fit and Where They Don’t** (Pietari Immonen) | Qualitative analysis of LLM impact on product-related work | Introduces the concept of **dual literacy**, combining domain expertise with the ability to prompt, evaluate, and refine AI-generated outputs. | **Supporting evidence** for emerging AI-enabled professional competencies. <br> **Theme:** Dual Literacy |
| **Leveraging AI-Powered Chatbots for Enhanced Requirements Elicitation and Documentation in Software Engineering** (Nicolae Zatic) | Conceptual discussion of AI-supported requirements engineering | Suggests AI automates repetitive tasks, increasing the value of critical thinking, stakeholder communication, negotiation, and strategic decision-making. | **Supporting evidence** for higher-order human competencies. <br> **Theme:** Human-Centric Competencies |
| **A Systematic Mapping of Large Language Models for Improving Natural Language Software Requirements** (Raiane Eunice C. Santos Fernandes, Fabio Gomes Rocha, Rheidner Achiles Couto S. Fernandes) | Systematic Mapping Study of 12 RE–LLM studies (2023–2025) | Identifies emerging RE competencies including prompt engineering, AI-assisted requirements analysis, validation, and human–AI collaboration. | **Supporting evidence** for foundational BA skills. <br> **Theme:** AI-Enhanced Requirements Engineering Skills |

# RQ2. How do current job postings in the software industry reflect the demand for AI-related skills in Business Analyst roles?

| Paper Title | Methodology / Data Source | Key Insights & Reason for Selection | Contribution to RQ / Emerging Theme |
|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| **Decoding the AI Job Market: Mapping Skills and Classifying Careers Through Online Job Ads** (Maayan Nakash, Or Peretz) | Analysis of **18,947 AI-related LinkedIn job postings** collected between January–September 2024 | Identifies **Software and Technology** as a major AI employment category (19.5%), including roles such as **Business Analyst**, Product Manager, and Strategy Consultant. Finds increasing demand for **hybrid skill profiles** combining technical skills (Python, Machine Learning, SQL, AWS) with socio-emotional competencies (Communication, Leadership, Problem Solving). Communication is the most frequently requested skill (50.93%), exceeding Python (44.01%) and Machine Learning (41.74%). Demonstrates that AI adoption increasingly requires professionals capable of bridging business and technical domains. | **Primary evidence** from real-world recruitment data. <br> **Theme:** Hybrid Skills <br> **Theme:** Business–Technology Bridging Role |
| **Preparing Business Analysts for the Modern Job Market: A Comparative Analysis of Skills and Education** (Agnieszka Kowalska-Styczeń, Kinga Juszczyk) | Labor market analysis and educational comparison | Reports increasing demand for BAs capable of advanced data analysis and managing digital technologies, including AI-related systems. | **Supporting evidence** confirming labor market trends. <br> **Theme:** Growing Demand for AI-Enabled BAs |

# RQ3. What gap exists between the skills highlighted in academic literature and those required in real-world BA job postings?

| Paper Title | Methodology / Data Source | Key Insights & Reason for Selection | Contribution to RQ / Emerging Theme |
|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| **Preparing Business Analysts for the Modern Job Market: A Comparative Analysis of Skills and Education** (Agnieszka Kowalska-Styczeń, Kinga Juszczyk) | Comparison of academic curricula and labor market expectations | Identifies a mismatch between educational preparation and rapidly evolving industry requirements, particularly regarding digital transformation competencies. | **Primary evidence** directly addressing the academia–industry gap. <br> **Theme:** Academia–Industry Gap |
| **Generative AI for Requirements Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review** (Haowei Cheng et al.) | Systematic Literature Review (SLR) | Finds that 90.3% of studies remain at conceptual or proof-of-concept stages, while only 1.3% focus on mature industrial deployment, indicating a maturity gap between research and practice. | **Primary evidence** for research–practice maturity gap. <br> **Theme:** Academia–Industry Gap |
| **Decoding the AI Job Market: Mapping Skills and Classifying Careers Through Online Job Ads** (Maayan Nakash, Or Peretz) | Analysis of 18,947 LinkedIn AI job postings | Shows that employers place strong emphasis on communication, leadership, and cross-functional collaboration alongside technical AI competencies. Communication is the most requested AI-related skill. | **Supporting evidence** showing industry priorities differ from the predominantly technical focus of academic discussions. <br> **Theme:** Human-Centric Competencies |
| **Recommendations for Efficient and Responsible LLM Adoption within Industrial Software Development** (Krishna Ronanki et al.) | Industrial adoption recommendations | Focuses on practical AI adoption capabilities such as prompt engineering, AI evaluation, and domain understanding, which are often underrepresented in traditional BA education. | **Supporting evidence** for emerging skill gaps. <br> **Theme:** AI Literacy Gap |

# Cross-RQ Theme Analysis

| Theme | Description | Supporting Papers |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Traditional BA Skills** | Analytical thinking, communication, project management, teamwork, and stakeholder engagement remain fundamental. | Kowalska-Styczeń & Juszczyk |
| **AI Literacy** | Professionals increasingly require understanding of LLMs, prompt engineering, AI evaluation, and responsible AI use. | Ronanki et al.; Immonen |
| **Dual Literacy** | Effective professionals combine domain expertise with AI interaction and evaluation capabilities. | Immonen |
| **Hybrid Skills** | Technical AI competencies must be complemented by communication, leadership, and business understanding. | Nakash & Peretz; Ronanki et al. |
| **Human-Centric Competencies** | Critical thinking, stakeholder communication, leadership, negotiation, and strategic decision-making remain essential despite AI adoption. | Zatic; Nakash & Peretz |
| **Business–Technology Bridging Role** | AI-enabled BAs increasingly act as intermediaries between business objectives and technical implementation. | Nakash & Peretz |
| **Academia–Industry Gap** | Academic research and educational curricula lag behind rapidly evolving industry requirements. | Kowalska-Styczeń & Juszczyk; Cheng et al. |
| **Growing Demand for AI-Enabled BAs** | Industry demand is increasing for Business Analysts who can work effectively with AI-supported technologies and processes. | Nakash & Peretz; Kowalska-Styczeń & Juszczyk |
