## Problem

The thesis investigates current Requirements Engineering (RE) practices and challenges within a case company and explores whether Artificial Intelligence, specifically Large Language Models (LLMs), can be applied to improve Requirements Engineering activities. The research focuses on three problem areas commonly encountered in industrial settings:

- Poor requirements traceability coverage.
- Inconsistent requirements quality.
- Labor-intensive requirements extraction processes.

The study seeks to determine whether AI-based solutions can improve productivity and effectiveness within Research and Development (R&D) activities.

## Method

The study employs a **mixed approach consisting of a literature review and an empirical case study**.

The research includes:

- A review of existing literature on LLM applications in Requirements Engineering.
- Development and evaluation of three AI-based solutions.
- Assessment of the solutions within the context of a real industrial case company.
- Analysis of both technical performance and practical applicability.

The evaluated solutions target:

- Requirements traceability creation.
- Requirements quality review.
- Requirements extraction.

## Context

The study was conducted within an industrial R&D environment.

The case company faced challenges related to:

- Limited traceability coverage.
- Variations in requirement quality.
- Significant manual effort required for extracting requirements from documentation and other sources.

The thesis evaluates how LLM-based approaches can support practical Requirements Engineering activities in a real organizational setting rather than solely in laboratory or academic environments.

## Key Findings

- AI demonstrates strong potential for supporting Requirements Engineering activities.

- LLMs can effectively support requirements traceability creation.

- Existing research indicates that LLMs often outperform traditional Information Retrieval (IR), Machine Learning (ML), and Deep Learning (DL) approaches in traceability-related tasks.

- Prompt engineering techniques such as:
  - Chain-of-thought prompting
  - RICE prompting
  - Context-aware prompting
  
  can significantly improve performance.

- LLMs show promising capabilities in requirements extraction and can help identify relevant requirements from stakeholder discussions, user reviews, regulations, and other textual sources.

- AI-based solutions can reduce manual effort associated with requirements analysis and documentation activities.

- The evaluated solution for requirements quality review did not achieve satisfactory practical performance within the case company.

- LLM outputs remain sensitive to:
  - Input data quality.
  - Prompt design.
  - Domain context.

- Human expertise remains necessary for validation, interpretation, and decision-making.

- The findings suggest that AI should be viewed as a support tool that augments Requirements Engineering activities rather than replacing Requirements Engineers or Business Analysts.

## Limitations

- The empirical evaluation was conducted within a single case company.

- The findings may not generalize to all industries or organizational contexts.

- The performance of LLM-based solutions depends heavily on data quality and contextual information.

- Some approaches require significant customization and prompt engineering effort before deployment.

- Rapid advancements in LLM technology may affect the long-term validity of specific tool evaluations.

- The study evaluates only selected AI-based solutions and does not represent the full range of available LLM technologies.

## Relevance to our topic

This thesis is highly relevant to the research topic because it examines how AI tools are being applied to Requirements Engineering activities that are commonly associated with Business Analyst responsibilities.

The findings contribute directly to **RQ1** by identifying competencies that become important when working with AI-assisted Requirements Engineering processes, including:

- Prompt engineering.
- AI output evaluation.
- Requirements traceability management.
- Requirements extraction using AI tools.
- Human oversight and validation.

The thesis also provides evidence that AI adoption does not eliminate the need for human expertise. Instead, professionals must combine domain knowledge with the ability to effectively evaluate and supervise AI-generated outputs.

For **RQ3**, the study highlights the difference between promising research results and practical deployment challenges, demonstrating that successful industrial adoption depends on data quality, context adaptation, and human involvement.
