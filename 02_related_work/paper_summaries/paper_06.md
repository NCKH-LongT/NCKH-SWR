# Paper 06 Summary

## Citation

Title: A Systematic Mapping of Large Language Models for Improving Natural Language Software Requirements

Authors: Raiane Eunice C. Santos Fernandes; Fabio Gomes Rocha; Rheidner Achiles Couto S. Fernandes

Year: 2026

Source: Proceedings of the 28th International Conference on Enterprise Information Systems (ICEIS 2026), Volume 2, pp. 1340–1351

DOI/Link: https://doi.org/10.5220/0014819100004018

## Problem

The paper investigates how Large Language Models (LLMs) have been applied to improve the quality of software requirements written in natural language.

The authors aim to understand how LLMs support the evaluation, refinement, and improvement of requirements quality, identify the techniques and tools used, examine the quality attributes addressed, evaluate empirical evidence, and explore challenges and limitations associated with LLM adoption in Requirements Engineering.

## Method

The study employs a Systematic Mapping Study (SMS).

The authors followed the methodology proposed by Petersen et al. and conducted searches across ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Scopus, and Web of Science. After applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, quality assessment, and snowballing procedures, 12 primary studies published between 2023 and 2025 were selected for detailed analysis.

The review examined LLM models, prompt engineering strategies, requirements quality attributes, evaluation methods, empirical evidence, limitations, and human involvement in LLM-supported Requirements Engineering activities.

## Context

The study was conducted within the field of Requirements Engineering and Software Engineering.

The reviewed studies focused on requirements expressed in natural language and investigated the use of LLMs for activities such as requirements elicitation, specification, analysis, validation, quality assurance, defect detection, ambiguity identification, inconsistency detection, and requirements refinement.

Most analyzed studies were conducted in academic or experimental settings using publicly available datasets, synthetic requirements, or researcher-created requirements rather than industrial projects.

## Key Findings

The study found that LLMs are being applied across multiple Requirements Engineering activities, including elicitation, specification, analysis, validation, and quality assurance.

GPT-family models, particularly GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, were the dominant technologies used in the reviewed studies, indicating strong reliance on proprietary LLMs.

The most frequently addressed requirements quality attributes were unambiguity and completeness, suggesting that current research primarily focuses on improving textual clarity and identifying missing information.

Evidence from the reviewed studies indicates that LLMs can support the detection of ambiguities, inconsistencies, redundancies, and missing requirements, while also assisting in requirements refinement and quality improvement.

The review found that prompt engineering plays a central role in LLM performance. Zero-shot prompting was the most commonly used strategy, although some studies employed few-shot prompting, chain-of-thought prompting, role-based prompting, and prompt chaining techniques.

The findings suggest that LLMs can reduce manual effort in requirements-related tasks and provide useful support for requirements generation, evaluation, and validation.

Human involvement remained important across most studies, with experts commonly serving as evaluators, validators, or providers of reference requirements. Only a small number of studies explored deeper human-AI collaboration models.

## Limitations

Most reviewed studies were conducted in academic or experimental environments rather than real industrial settings, limiting the generalizability of the findings.

The literature lacks standardized datasets containing labeled requirements quality defects, creating challenges for reproducibility and comparison across studies.

Most studies relied on English-language requirements, resulting in limited evidence regarding multilingual Requirements Engineering contexts.

The reviewed research focused primarily on textual quality attributes such as ambiguity and completeness, while other quality attributes received significantly less attention.

The study identified substantial variability in evaluation methods, metrics, datasets, and prompt engineering approaches, making cross-study comparisons difficult.

The mapping included only 12 primary studies that satisfied the selection criteria, reflecting the emerging nature of this research area.

## Relevance to our topic

This paper is highly relevant to RQ1, has limited relevance to RQ2, and provides indirect support for RQ3.

For RQ1, the paper contributes valuable evidence regarding AI-related competencies associated with Requirements Engineering activities that overlap substantially with Business Analyst responsibilities. The review highlights the growing importance of understanding Large Language Models, prompt engineering techniques, AI-assisted requirements elicitation, requirements analysis, requirements validation, requirements quality assessment, and human-AI collaboration. These competencies represent emerging knowledge areas that may become increasingly important for Business Analysts working in software development environments where AI tools are adopted.

For RQ2, the contribution is limited because the study does not analyze job advertisements, labor market demand, recruitment practices, or Business Analyst hiring requirements. The evidence is derived exclusively from academic literature.

For RQ3, the paper provides a useful academic baseline that can be compared with findings from Business Analyst job postings. The AI-related capabilities emphasized in the literature, such as prompt engineering, LLM-assisted requirements analysis, AI-supported validation, and understanding AI limitations, can later be compared against industry hiring requirements to identify potential gaps between academic recommendations and real-world employer expectations.

Overall, the paper serves as a strong academic source for identifying AI-related skills and competencies connected to Requirements Engineering activities that are commonly performed by Business Analysts in software development projects.
