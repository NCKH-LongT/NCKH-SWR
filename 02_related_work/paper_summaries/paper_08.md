# Paper 08 Summary

## Citation

**Title:**  
Large Language Model for Requirements Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review

**Authors:**  
Javed Ali Khan, Shamaila Qayyum, Hafsa Shareef Dar

**Year:**  
2024

**Source:**  
Research Square Preprint

**DOI/Link:**  
https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-5589929/v1

## Problem

The paper investigates how Large Language Models (LLMs) are being applied within Requirements Engineering (RE). The authors aim to identify the RE activities supported by LLMs, examine the techniques used to implement LLM-based solutions, analyze evaluation methods adopted by researchers, and highlight current research gaps and future opportunities in the field.

## Method

The study employs a **Systematic Literature Review (SLR)**.

The authors systematically searched and analyzed research publications related to the application of LLMs in Requirements Engineering. After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a final set of **35 primary studies** was selected for analysis.

The selected studies were examined according to:

- Requirements Engineering activities supported
- LLM techniques and prompting strategies
- Evaluation approaches and metrics
- Types of requirements artifacts
- Research trends and challenges

The findings were synthesized to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of LLM adoption in Requirements Engineering.

## Context

The study was conducted within the context of **Software Requirements Engineering**.

The reviewed literature investigated the use of LLMs across several Requirements Engineering activities, including:

- Requirements elicitation
- Requirements analysis
- Requirements modelling
- Requirements specification
- Requirements validation
- Requirements prioritization
- Requirements traceability

The studies addressed a variety of requirements-related artifacts, including software requirements documents, user stories, stakeholder inputs, issue reports, and other textual requirements sources.

Most studies focused on experimental or research-oriented environments rather than large-scale industrial deployments.

## Key Findings

- Research on LLMs in Requirements Engineering has increased significantly following the emergence of modern generative AI technologies.

- Requirements elicitation and requirements analysis are among the most frequently studied RE activities.

- LLMs are increasingly used to automate or support:
  - Requirements extraction
  - Requirements classification
  - Requirements prioritization
  - Requirements validation
  - Requirements traceability

- Prompt engineering plays a central role in enabling LLM-based Requirements Engineering solutions.

- Researchers commonly employ:
  - Zero-shot prompting
  - Few-shot prompting
  - Customized prompt strategies

- GPT-based models dominate the literature and represent the most frequently used LLM family.

- LLMs demonstrate strong potential for improving productivity in Requirements Engineering activities involving large volumes of textual information.

- Evaluation of LLM-based approaches commonly relies on metrics such as:
  - Precision
  - Recall
  - F1-score
  - Accuracy
  - Human expert assessment

- Despite promising results, current applications remain largely experimental and are not yet widely integrated into industrial software development workflows.

## Limitations

- The review includes only studies identified through the selected search strategy and databases.

- The number of available studies remains relatively limited because LLM adoption in Requirements Engineering is still an emerging research area.

- Most reviewed studies focus on controlled experimental settings rather than real-world industrial environments.

- GPT-based models dominate the literature, limiting understanding of alternative LLM architectures.

- The rapid evolution of LLM technologies may affect the long-term validity of some findings.

## Relevance to our topic

This paper is relevant to the research topic because Requirements Engineering activities are closely associated with responsibilities commonly performed by Business Analysts in software development projects.

The review demonstrates that AI technologies are increasingly supporting activities such as requirements elicitation, analysis, prioritization, validation, and traceability.

The findings contribute directly to **RQ1** by identifying AI-related competencies that may become increasingly important for Business Analysts working in AI-supported software development environments, including prompt engineering, AI-assisted analysis, and evaluation of AI-generated outputs.

The paper also supports **RQ3** by highlighting that most current research remains concentrated in experimental settings, while practical industrial adoption and workflow integration remain relatively limited. This suggests a gap between academic research and real-world implementation.
