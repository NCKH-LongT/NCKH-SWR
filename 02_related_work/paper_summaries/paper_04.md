# Paper 04 Summary

## Citation

Title: Decoding the AI Job Market: Mapping Skills and Classifying Careers Through Online Job Ads

Authors: Maayan Nakash; Or Peretz

Year: 2025

Source: Employee Relations: The International Journal, pp. 1–18

DOI/Link: https://doi.org/10.1108/ER-07-2025-0566

## Problem

The paper investigates how AI-related skills are distributed across occupations and how AI careers can be classified using online job advertisements. It aims to identify the skills employers demand in AI-related positions and examine the relationship between occupational categories and required competencies.

## Method

The study adopts a quantitative research design based on online job advertisements collected from LinkedIn.

AI-related job postings containing the terms "artificial intelligence" and "AI" were analyzed using text mining and natural language processing techniques. The authors extracted skills from job advertisements, classified occupations into categories, and applied chi-square tests, Random Forest classification, and Information Gain analysis to examine relationships between skills and job categories.

## Context

The study was conducted using AI-related job advertisements published on LinkedIn across multiple industries and occupational categories. The dataset reflects employer demand for AI talent in the labor market and includes technical and non-technical roles such as Data and Analytics, Business and Operations, Creative and Media, Education and Human Development, Customer and Support Services, and Career Development.

## Key Findings

The study found that Data and Analytics positions represented more than one-third of all AI-related job postings, while Business and Operations roles accounted for 29.3% of the sample.

Python was identified as the most influential skill for distinguishing AI-related job categories, followed by machine learning, statistics, PyTorch, and deep learning.

Communication was identified as an important skill across multiple occupational categories, indicating that employers value both technical expertise and transferable soft skills.

The analysis revealed that AI-related competencies are increasingly required outside traditional technical roles, demonstrating the diffusion of AI capabilities into business, operational, educational, and creative functions.

Statistically significant relationships were found between job categories and 101 of the 104 analyzed skills.

## Limitations

The study analyzed only LinkedIn job advertisements containing the keywords "artificial intelligence" and "AI," which may have excluded relevant AI-related positions using different terminology.

The dataset was limited to a single recruitment platform and therefore may not fully represent the broader labor market.

The research focused on employer demand reflected in job advertisements and did not examine employee skills, hiring outcomes, workforce adaptation, or organizational implementation of AI.

The study did not specifically focus on Business Analyst roles or software development environments.

## Relevance to our topic

This paper contributes primarily to RQ2 and RQ3.

For RQ1, the contribution is limited because the study does not specifically examine Business Analysts or software development environments. However, it identifies AI-related technical skills such as Python, machine learning, deep learning, statistics, and PyTorch, as well as soft skills such as communication, which may serve as reference skills when reviewing the literature on AI-enabled Business Analyst competencies.

For RQ2, the paper provides strong evidence because it analyzes real-world job advertisements from LinkedIn. The findings demonstrate how employers communicate AI-related skill requirements through job postings and show that AI competencies are increasingly demanded across both technical and business-oriented occupations. The methodology can also inform the analysis of Business Analyst job postings in the software industry.

For RQ3, the paper offers a practical industry perspective that can be compared with findings from academic literature. The skill demands identified in job advertisements provide empirical evidence that can be used to evaluate whether skills emphasized in the literature are reflected in real-world hiring requirements. This supports the identification of potential gaps between academic recommendations and industry expectations.

Overall, the paper is most useful as an industry-demand reference for examining AI-related skills in job advertisements and for supporting comparisons between academic literature and labor market requirements. Its relevance is indirect because it focuses on AI occupations broadly rather than Business Analyst roles specifically.
