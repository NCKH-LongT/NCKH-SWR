# Paper 02 Summary

## Citation

- **Title:** Preparing Business Analysts for the Modern Job Market: A Comparative Analysis of Skills and Education
- **Author(s):** Agnieszka Kowalska-Styczeń, Kinga Juszczyk
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** Scientific Papers of Silesian University of Technology (Organization and Management Series No. 208), Silesian University of Technology Publishing House, Poland.
- **DOI/Link:** http://dx.doi.org/10.29119/1641-3466.2024.208.13

## Problem

The paper addresses the persistent misalignment and skill gaps between academic Business Analytics/Business Analysis university curricula and the rapidly evolving, tech-driven demands of the modern labor market. In the current era of digital transformation, the role of a BA has fundamentally shifted from a traditional requirement-gatherer to a strategic partner driving the integration of emerging technologies like Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), and Big Data into business operations.

## Method

The study utilized a comparative qualitative and quantitative analysis of higher education curricula (Curricula Comparative Analysis):

1. **Data Sampling:** Selected 10 prominent business analysis and data analytics degree programs across top-tier Polish universities, evenly split between 5 Bachelor of Science/Economics degrees and 5 Engineering degrees.
2. **Academic Skill Mapping:** Extracted and mapped all specialized course modules into 4 industry-standard core competency pillars derived from professional frameworks (such as the PMI Guide to Business Analysis): Analytical, Technical, Communication, and Project Management skills.
3. **Labor Market Correlation:** Integrated secondary industry data—including the Bulldogjob 2024 IT community report, Google Trends historical data (2004–2024), and national wage statistics (Wynagrodzenia.pl)—to evaluate market demand dynamics and tool-specific adoption trends.

## Context

The research is contextualized within the Polish higher education system and its domestic IT labor market during 2021–2024. This period marks a major global digitalization wave, which triggered a 2.5-fold increase in BA job advertisements and pushed average salaries up by approximately 17% for both junior and senior roles.

## Key Findings

1. The organizational role of the Business Analyst has underwent an evolutionary shift: transitioning from an Intermediary (mediating communication) into a Strategic Partner who directly shapes corporate value and drives data-driven decision-making.
2. A distinct structural divergence exists between degree pathways:
   - **Bachelor's programs** deliver a broader, more balanced skill base, dedicating significant coursework to soft skills, interpersonal dynamics, and stakeholders management.
   - **Engineering programs** focus heavily on technical and analytical rigor (applied mathematics, Machine Learning, Data Engineering) but almost entirely neglect interpersonal communication and project governance within core modules.
3. Industry analysis identified the definitive stack of essential technological tools that a modern BA must master to remain employable: Microsoft Excel, Python, Microsoft Power BI, Tableau, Oracle, Microsoft SQL Server, and Enterprise Architect.

## Limitations

1. The empirical data collection was geographically bounded to universities within Poland, meaning some curriculum structures reflect local regional policies and require wider cross-border validation.
2. The curriculum evaluation relied solely on formal syllabus descriptions and secondary labor market indicators, lacking direct empirical primary data (such as qualitative interviews or surveys) from active corporate employers regarding graduate performance.

## Relevance to our topic

This study provides valuable theoretical and methodological support for **RQ2** and **RQ3**. The four-pillar competency framework (Analytical, Technical, Communication, and Project Management) offers a useful structure for analyzing the **3,745 IT job postings** in Vietnam. Furthermore, the findings provide supporting evidence that technical literacy is becoming increasingly important for Business Analysts. The study suggests that modern BAs must continuously develop technological competencies alongside communication and strategic skills to remain effective in an AI-augmented environment.
