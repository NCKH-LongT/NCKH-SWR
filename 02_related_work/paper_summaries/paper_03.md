# Paper 03 Summary

## Citation

**Title:**  
The Changing Nature of Product Management Work: Where Large Language Models Fit and Where They Don’t

**Authors:**  
Pietari Immonen

**Year:**  
2026

**Source:**  
Aalto University, School of Science, Master's Programme in Information Networks, Master's Thesis

**DOI/Link:**  
https://urn.fi/URN:NBN:fi:aalto-202605203907

## Problem

The thesis investigates how Large Language Models (LLMs) are changing product management work and seeks to determine where LLMs can effectively support product managers and where they remain inadequate. The study addresses the growing uncertainty surrounding the role of AI in product management and examines how professionals can integrate LLMs into their work while maintaining effectiveness in tasks that require human judgment, stakeholder interaction, and strategic thinking.

## Method

The study employs a **qualitative research approach** based on **semi-structured interviews**.

The research involved:

- Interviews with product management professionals.
- Thematic analysis of interview data.
- Comparison of practitioner experiences with existing literature on product management and LLM adoption.

The study explores how practitioners use LLMs in their daily work, what benefits they experience, and what limitations prevent broader adoption.

## Context

The research was conducted in the context of **product management within software and technology-intensive organizations**.

The study focuses on product managers working in environments where LLMs are becoming increasingly available for professional tasks such as:

- Information gathering
- Documentation
- Content generation
- Analysis support
- Ideation
- Communication support

The thesis examines how these technologies affect real-world product management activities and decision-making processes.

## Key Findings

- LLMs are most effective when supporting individual productivity and information-processing tasks.

- Product managers use LLMs for:
  - Drafting and refining documents.
  - Summarizing information.
  - Brainstorming and idea generation.
  - Learning unfamiliar topics.
  - Supporting communication-related activities.

- LLMs provide significant value in reducing the time required for repetitive and administrative tasks.

- Despite these benefits, LLMs are not well suited for activities requiring:
  - Deep contextual understanding.
  - Strategic decision-making.
  - Organizational influence.
  - Stakeholder management.
  - Negotiation.
  - Relationship building.

- The study finds that product management work contains many social and organizational dimensions that remain difficult to automate using current LLM technology.

- Participants emphasized the importance of combining domain expertise with the ability to effectively use and evaluate AI-generated outputs.

- The thesis introduces the concept of **dual literacy**, referring to the combination of:
  - Professional domain knowledge.
  - The capability to interact effectively with LLMs through prompting, evaluation, and iterative refinement.

- The findings suggest that future professionals will need both traditional professional expertise and AI-related competencies to remain effective.

## Limitations

- The study relies on a qualitative interview-based approach.

- The sample size is limited and may not represent all product management contexts.

- The rapid evolution of LLM technologies means that some findings may become outdated as new models and capabilities emerge.

- The study focuses on current-generation LLMs and does not account for future technological developments.

- Findings are based on participant perceptions and experiences rather than controlled experimental measurements.

## Relevance to our topic

This thesis is relevant to the research topic because it provides insights into the skills required for professionals working alongside AI systems in software-related environments.

Specifically, the study contributes to understanding:

- The growing importance of AI literacy.
- The need to evaluate and refine AI-generated outputs.
- The continued importance of human-centered competencies such as communication, stakeholder management, negotiation, and strategic thinking.
- The emergence of dual literacy as a combination of domain expertise and AI interaction skills.

The thesis contributes directly to **RQ1** by identifying competencies needed when professionals work with LLM-supported processes.

It also contributes to **RQ3** by highlighting that AI adoption does not eliminate the need for human expertise, suggesting a potential gap between technical AI capabilities and the broader skill set required in professional practice.
