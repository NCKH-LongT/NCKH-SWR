# Paper 03 Summary

## Citation

- **Title:** Dynamics of Changes in Competencies Required in the Labour Market for Data Analyst and Business Analyst Professions in Russia
- **Author(s):** Valeriia S. Arteeva, Angi E. Skhvediani, Sergey Sosnovskikh
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** Journal of Applied Economic Research (Vol. 23, No. 4, Pp. 1150-1181), Ural Federal University, Russia in collaboration with Manchester Metropolitan University, United Kingdom.
- **DOI/Link:** https://doi.org/10.15826/vestnik.2024.23.4.045

## Problem

The paper investigates the structural dynamics and evolving competencies demanded by employers for two pivotal Big Data Analytics (BDA) roles: Data Analyst (DA) and Business Analyst (BA). It tackles the knowledge gap surrounding the professional convergence and skill interpenetration of these two occupations driven by rapid digital transformation and severe macroeconomic shifts.

## Method

The authors adopted a rigorous, data-driven Multimethod Approach leveraging labor market big data over four distinct phases:

1. **Web-scraping and Data Collection:** Extracted the full-text content of 2,357 active online job vacancies for DA and BA roles across two comparative chronological checkpoints: 2020 (the outbreak of COVID-19) and 2023 (the post-pandemic digital acceleration era).
2. **Text Processing (NLP):** Applied Natural Language Processing techniques—including data parsing, tokenization, and n-gram extraction—to isolate explicit skill phrases and requirements from raw vacancy texts.
3. **Competency Architecture Modeling (SNA):** Used Social Network Analysis (SNA) to map the co-occurrence of skills within job advertisements. Skills mentioned together form nodes and edges, allowing the researchers to identify central competency clusters.
4. **Statistical and Network Visualization:** Conducted quantitative comparisons to measure shifts in the weight, density, and centrality scores of specific skill clusters between 2020 and 2023.

## Context

The study is set within the Russian digital labor market under intense macroeconomic stress. The market experienced double-forced transformation due to the post-COVID-19 digitalization push and the geopolitical events of 2022, which forced rapid import-substitution of Western software and a critical domestic demand for data-driven optimization.

## Key Findings

1. There is a powerful interdisciplinary convergence occurring between the two professions, blurring traditional operational boundaries:
   - **Data Analysts (DAs)** are increasingly required to absorb business acumen, organizational process knowledge, and interpersonal soft skills.
   - **Business Analysts (BAs)** are heavily pressured to acquire advanced technical skills in Big Data Analytics, including programming logic, data querying languages, and quantitative modeling.
2. Modern BA vacancies exhibit a massive structural shift toward system process modeling, corporate data architecture comprehension, and automated tool proficiency.
3. Interpersonal skills, specifically creative thinking and empathy, have elevated to central status in network maps, proving critical for BAs to orchestrate cross-functional collaboration in complex technology projects.

## Limitations

1. The web-scraped dataset is tied to the Russian economic landscape, which might exhibit localized distortions in specific software tools due to the mandated transition away from Western tech stacks to domestic software alternatives.
2. The data source is restricted to job postings, which capture the ideal expectations and hiring preferences of employers rather than the day-to-day granular tasks executed by practitioners in actual project environments.

## Relevance to our topic

This paper serves as an authoritative methodology blueprint for our group's core execution (**RQ2** and **RQ3**). The workflow of scraping job postings, applying NLP parsing, and mapping skill frequencies closely aligns with the methodology planned for analyzing the **3,745 IT job postings** dataset in Vietnam. In addition, the findings demonstrate a growing convergence between business and technical competencies. This supports our argument that, in an AI-driven environment, Business Analysts need not only communication and stakeholder management skills but also sufficient data and AI literacy to effectively bridge business goals and technological solutions.
