### 1. Automated Planning for Ubiquitous Computing

* **Link:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/epdf/10.1145/3004294
* **Paper Title:** Automated Planning for Ubiquitous Computing
* **Authors:** Ilche Georgievski, Marco Aiello
* **Source:** University of Groningen (ACM Digital Library)
* **Real-world Problem:** Implementing automated planning in ubiquitous computing environments is highly challenging because contextual data changes constantly and user behavior is inherently unpredictable.
* **Significance:** If the system cannot dynamically process and adapt to real-time changes in a user's schedule, the generated plans become obsolete instantly, making the reminder application completely ineffective.
* **Research Gap:** The study focuses heavily on macro-level computing infrastructure, failing to address specific engineering requirements tailored to behavioral psychology or mitigating student procrastination.

### 2. Student Academic Planner System: A Review

* **Link:** https://mjosht.usim.edu.my/index.php/mjosht/article/view/326/201
* **Paper Title:** Student Academic Planner System: A Review
* **Authors:** Fathi Amalin Mohamad Zukriyani, Nurdiana Azizan
* **Source:** Malaysian Journal of Science, Health & Technology (MJoSHT, USIM)
* **Real-world Problem:** Most university students still rely on manual note-taking or rudimentary reminder apps, which leads to highly inefficient management of exam schedules and major assignments.
* **Significance:** The lack of an integrated smart academic management system increases the rate of late submissions, causing severe pressure for both students and academic administrators.
* **Research Gap:** The paper is limited to a high-level review of traditional planning systems and does not establish functional requirements for leveraging AI or Large Language Models (LLMs) to provide interactive support.

### 3. Implementation of Octalysis-Based Gamification Method Cross-Platform for Design of Time Management Application

* **Link:** https://www.ijcs.net/ijcs/index.php/ijcs/article/view/3568/443
* **Paper Title:** Implementation of Octalysis-Based Gamification Method Cross-Platform for Design of Time Management Application
* **Authors:** Kurnia Christopher, Fenina Adline Twince Tobing
* **Source:** Indonesian Journal of Computer Science (IJCS)
* **Real-world Problem:** Standard software applications struggle to sustain long-term user engagement due to a lack of core mechanisms that stimulate intrinsic motivation.
* **Significance:** For highly procrastinating students, if a study reminder application lacks engaging elements (such as gamification), they will quickly dismiss notifications or uninstall the app within a few days.
* **Research Gap:** The research centers strictly on the general Octalysis gamification framework without combining requirements engineering to synchronize gamified features with AI algorithms for automated schedule recommendations.

### 4. Struggling to Keep Tabs on Capstone Projects: A Chatbot to Tackle Student Procrastination

* **Link:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/epdf/10.1145/3469127
* **Paper Title:** Struggling to Keep Tabs on Capstone Projects: A Chatbot to Tackle Student Procrastination
* **Authors:** Juanan Pereira, Óscar Díaz
* **Source:** University of the Basque Country, UPV/EHU (ACM Digital Library)
* **Real-world Problem:** Designing conversational agents (chatbots) for task management often results in rigid, robotic interactions that fail to understand the deep psychological barriers and context leading to student disengagement during capstone projects.
* **Significance:** An intelligent virtual assistant must know how to "negotiate" and deliver adaptive guidance based on a student's fatigue or procrastination status, rather than just blasting hardcoded, repetitive alerts.
* **Research Gap:** The study focuses narrowly on monitoring milestones for capstone projects, leaving a gap in functional requirements engineering for daily schedule management and integration with digital entertainment platforms.

### 5. Development of a Mobile Intervention for Procrastination Augmented With a Semigenerative Chatbot for University Students: Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial

* **Link:** https://mhealth.jmir.org/2025/1/e53133/
* **Paper Title:** Development of a Mobile Intervention for Procrastination Augmented With a Semigenerative Chatbot for University Students: Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial
* **Authors:** Seonmi Lee, Jaehyun Jeong, Myungsung Kim, Sangil Lee, Sung-Phil Kim, Dooyoung Jung
* **Source:** JMIR mHealth and uHealth (Published 2025)
* **Real-world Problem:** Modern university students face immense difficulty balancing digital lifestyles (social media, entertainment) with academic timelines, resulting in chronic deadline failures.
* **Significance:** This lifestyle imbalance accumulates over time, causing severe academic stress, reducing deep focus capacity, and lowering the overall quality of learning.
* **Research Gap:** The virtual coach application utilizes a rigid, semi-generative chatbot system, failing to exploit requirements engineering for fully generative AI (LLMs) that can completely automate schedule synchronization with official university academic portals.
