# Paper 07 Summary

## Citation

**Title:**  
The Role of Generative AI Models in Requirements Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review

**Authors:**  
Poonkuzhali Vasudevan, Sandeep Reddivari

**Year:**  
2025

**Source:**  
Proceedings of the 2025 ACM Southeast Conference (ACMSE 2025), Pages 188–194

**DOI/Link:**  
https://doi.org/10.1145/3696673.3723053

## Problem

The paper investigates the current state of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) adoption in Requirements Engineering (RE). The authors aim to identify available GenAI models used in RE, understand how these models support RE activities, evaluate the evidence supporting their adoption, and analyze the limitations of current GenAI applications in RE.

## Method

The study employs a **Systematic Literature Review (SLR)**.

The review was guided by three research questions:

- RQ1: Are there any GenAI models available that aid in RE activities?
- RQ2: How much evidence is available to utilize GenAI models in RE?
- RQ3: What are the limitations of current applications of GenAI models in RE? 

The search process included:

- Automatic searches in ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, and Springer.
- Manual searches using Google Scholar.
- Literature published between 2013 and 2024.
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria applied independently by two reviewers.
- Final selection of 44 primary studies. 

The reviewers achieved a 95% agreement rate during the study selection process. 

## Context

The study was conducted in the context of **Software Requirements Engineering**.

The reviewed literature examined the use of Generative AI technologies, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), across various RE activities including:

- Requirements elicitation
- Requirements analysis
- Requirements specification
- Requirements validation
- Requirements management

The review analyzed studies from both academic and industrial contexts involving software requirements documents, user stories, use cases, contracts, interview scripts, app reviews, and other requirements-related artifacts.

## Key Findings

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are the dominant GenAI technology used in Requirements Engineering.

- BERT was the most frequently used model, appearing in approximately 45% of the reviewed studies, while GPT-based models accounted for approximately 38%. 

- The identified models support all major Requirements Engineering activities:
  - Elicitation
  - Analysis
  - Specification
  - Validation
  - Management 

- Requirements analysis was the most commonly supported activity, followed by specification and elicitation, while validation and management received comparatively less attention. 

- LLMs were capable of processing multiple requirements artifacts, including:
  - Requirements documents
  - User stories
  - Use cases
  - Obligation statements
  - Elicitation interview scripts
  - Application reviews 

- Common approaches used in the literature included:
  - Prompt engineering
  - Prompt patterns
  - Fine-tuning
  - Transfer learning
  - Zero-shot learning
  - NLP-based preprocessing techniques 

- Regarding evidence strength:
  - 39% of studies relied on academic studies.
  - 30% relied on demonstrations.
  - 18% relied on industrial studies.
  - 9% relied on expert opinion or observation. 

- The review concluded that GenAI models, particularly LLMs, demonstrate significant versatility and applicability across multiple Requirements Engineering activities. 

## Limitations

- The reviewed studies primarily focused on two approaches:
  - Training transformer-based models such as BERT for RE tasks.
  - Using LLMs such as GPT through prompt engineering.

- The literature showed limited exploration of alternative GenAI models and techniques beyond these approaches. 

- The search strategy relied on a predefined search string, which may have excluded relevant studies that used different terminology. 

- The review examined only the selected primary studies and did not analyze references cited within those studies, potentially omitting additional relevant literature. 

- The authors acknowledge that more effective GenAI tools or techniques may exist but were either not identified through the search process or have not yet been developed or published. 

## Relevance to our topic

This paper is relevant to the research topic because it provides a comprehensive overview of how Generative AI and Large Language Models are currently being applied within Requirements Engineering, one of the core responsibilities frequently associated with Business Analyst roles in software development environments.

The review demonstrates that AI technologies are increasingly supporting requirements elicitation, analysis, specification, and validation activities, indicating a growing need for professionals to understand how these tools can be effectively applied in practice.

The findings contribute to **RQ1** by identifying AI-related capabilities and practices associated with modern Requirements Engineering activities.

The paper also contributes to **RQ3** by highlighting the gap between current academic experimentation and broader industrial adoption, as well as identifying limitations that may affect the practical use of GenAI technologies in professional environments.
