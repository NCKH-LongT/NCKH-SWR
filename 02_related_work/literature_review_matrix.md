# Literature Review Matrix

Below is the literature review matrix analyzing the 7 fundamental research papers used as the scientific foundation for this study on requirements engineering for a RAG-based LMS that generates CLO-aligned quizzes:

| No | Paper | Year | Venue | Topic / Domain | Research Method | Context | Key Findings | Limitation | Relevance to Our Topic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paper 01 — Chen & Shiu (2025) | 2025 | arXiv / IEEE TechRxiv | Difficulty-controlled question generation for educational assessment | System design and experiment with human benchmarking and simulation study | Professional certification exams at Taiwan's National Institute of Environmental Research | Integrating Bloom's Taxonomy and IRT into a RAG pipeline enables stable difficulty control with only 6.7% misclassification; system reduced manual workload in certification exam contexts | Multi-agent DDS architecture is too complex for lightweight LMS integration; cognitive levels are encoded as internal parameters with no instructor-facing CLO selection step | Reveals that cognitive alignment requirements are treated as system parameters rather than formally specified stakeholder requirements, highlighting the absence of functional requirements for CLO selection and question tagging |
| 2 | Paper 02 — Maity et al. (2025) | 2025 | arXiv (FIRE 2025) | Automated question generation in multi-domain school education | Comparative experiment with automated and human evaluation (Fleiss's Kappa) | Indian secondary school education using NCERT textbooks (grades 6–12) | Hybrid model combining FAISS RAG and GPT-4 few-shot ICL outperforms all baselines on pedagogical criteria; 5-criterion rubric (grammaticality, relevance, complexity, answerability) validated by teachers | No instructor review or edit interface before publishing; CLO alignment identified as a needed next step but not implemented | The 5-criterion pedagogical rubric implicitly defines instructor validation requirements for quiz quality; the missing review interface is explicitly acknowledged as an unspecified functional requirement |
| 3 | Paper 03 — Hamidi et al. (2025) | 2025 | Journal of Advances in Information Technology (JAIT) | Automated exam creation with RAG for higher education | System design with expert validation and small classroom pilot | Higher education SQL database courses in Morocco (French-language context) | RAG pipeline with zero-shot re-ranking achieved 85% Precision@5 and reduced instructor exam preparation time by 38%; human-in-the-loop review interface built in Next.js | Evaluation lacks automated RAG quality frameworks (e.g., Ragas); ~10% schema deviation and ~15% difficulty misalignment observed but handling not specified; limited to French and SQL only | Describes an instructor review workflow in practice but does not formalize it as functional requirements; schema deviation and misalignment findings motivate the need for formally specified acceptance criteria and rejection handling |
| 4 | Paper 04 — Reddig et al. (2025) | 2025 | Springer (Int. Journal of AI in Education) | Personalized feedback generation in Intelligent Tutoring Systems | Experiment with error classification, expert human evaluation, and automated simulated student evaluation | College Algebra course using the Apprentice Tutors ITS platform (USA) | GPT-4 diagnosed student errors with ~80% accuracy for single errors; 35% of generated hints were too general, incorrect, or leaked answers; only 35% of feedback passed simulated student usefulness test | 35% error rate too high for real-world deployment; no RAG grounding for course materials; experiments limited to a highly structured mathematics subject | Quantifies the consequences of missing non-functional requirements for feedback quality thresholds; demonstrates that deploying LLM feedback without formally specified acceptance criteria results in unacceptable error rates |
| 5 | Paper 05 — Scaria et al. (2024) | 2024 | AIED 2024 (Springer LNAI) | Bloom's Taxonomy-aligned question generation using LLMs | Comparative experiment with expert human evaluation and LLM-based evaluation (Cohen's Kappa) | Graduate-level Data Science education (17 topics, India context) | PS4 prompting strategy (CoT + Bloom definitions + few-shot examples) produced best results; 78% of questions rated high quality; 65.56% accurately mapped to requested Bloom level; automated LLM evaluation not on par with human evaluation | No RAG — relies entirely on LLM internal knowledge, risking hallucination; LLM-based automated evaluation correlates poorly with expert judgments | Demonstrates that instructor-validated quality criteria for Bloom-aligned questions exist and can be systematically applied; confirms that automated evaluation cannot replace stakeholder validation, directly informing requirements for human-in-the-loop review |
| 6 | Paper 06 — Modi et al. (2023) | 2023 | IEEE/ACM ICSE-SEET 2023 | CLO-aligned personalized feedback in Software Engineering education | Case study with small cohort evaluation using the Focus framework | Undergraduate software engineering courses in Australia | Focus framework successfully relates feedback across tasks to CLO performance; students gain clearer understanding of learning trajectory; personalized feedback improves reflection efficiency | Initial CLO-to-task mapping requires significant instructor effort; evaluated in limited class cohorts; no AI or automation involved | Establishes CLO-aligned feedback as a genuine and valued instructor requirement in SE education; the high manual effort confirms the need to formally specify functional requirements for automating CLO mapping and feedback generation |
| 7 | Paper 07 — Bui Thanh Khoa et al. (2020) | 2020 | HCMCOU Journal of Science | LMS adoption by lecturers in Vietnamese higher education | Mixed methods: online group discussions and TAM2-based survey | Vietnamese university lecturers across multiple institutions during COVID-19 context | Output quality, result demonstrability, ease of use, and workload reduction are significant predictors of LMS adoption intention; lecturer workload in creating digital content is a major adoption barrier | Study does not evaluate AI-assisted features or technical enhancements; findings are survey-based and limited to specific Vietnamese universities | Provides empirically validated non-functional requirements for LMS adoption in Vietnam — output quality, usability, and workload reduction — that have not been translated into formal NFRs for AI-assisted LMS systems |
