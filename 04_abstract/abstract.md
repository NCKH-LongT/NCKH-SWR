
# Abstract

Academic procrastination has become an increasingly significant challenge in higher education due to the rapid growth of digital distractions, particularly short-form social media platforms. These platforms encourage instant gratification, reduce students' attention spans, and negatively affect their ability to manage study time effectively. Although Artificial Intelligence (AI) has recently been adopted in educational technologies to improve learning support, existing research primarily investigates isolated solutions such as procrastination prediction, intelligent scheduling, conversational agents, or time-management applications. Consequently, there remains a lack of an integrated AI-driven system capable of addressing academic procrastination comprehensively.

This study aims to establish the research foundation for developing an AI-driven personal assistant that helps students mitigate academic procrastination and improve time management through adaptive, personalized, and explainable support. Specifically, the study seeks to identify the functional and non-functional requirements, research gaps, and technological opportunities for building a trustworthy AI-based educational assistant suitable for university students, particularly within the Vietnamese higher education context.

To achieve this objective, the research employs a structured literature review of representative studies on academic procrastination, educational AI, Large Language Models (LLMs), intelligent tutoring systems, machine learning prediction models, and Requirements Engineering for AI-enabled software. The reviewed literature is analyzed comparatively to identify common research themes, existing limitations, and unresolved challenges relevant to the proposed system.

The analysis reveals several important findings. Current studies typically focus on only one capability instead of providing an end-to-end solution. Furthermore, Requirements Engineering practices for AI educational assistants remain insufficient, explainability and transparency are rarely incorporated into recommendation mechanisms, and most evaluations rely on small-scale or short-term experiments without validation in Southeast Asian educational settings. These limitations highlight the need for a comprehensive AI assistant that integrates behavioral analytics, adaptive scheduling, procrastination prediction, conversational coaching, and explainable AI within a unified architecture.

This study contributes a consolidated understanding of the current research landscape, synthesizes major research gaps, formulates research questions, and establishes a clear problem statement for future system development. The findings provide a solid Requirements Engineering foundation for designing and validating an AI-driven personal assistant capable of supporting sustainable study habits, improving time-management skills, and reducing academic procrastination among university students.
