## 3. Research Domain

Software Engineering · Requirements Engineering · Business Analysis · Artificial Intelligence · Literature Review

This study investigates how the adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), is changing the competency requirements of Business Analysts (BAs) in software development. It focuses on identifying AI-related skills discussed in academic literature and comparing them with skills currently expected by industry.

---

## 4. Problem Statement

Business Analysts play an essential role in software development by eliciting, analyzing, documenting, and validating software requirements while acting as a bridge between business stakeholders and development teams.

The increasing adoption of AI and Large Language Models is transforming many Requirements Engineering activities, including requirements elicitation, analysis, validation, traceability, and documentation. Recent studies show that AI can improve productivity and support decision-making, but also introduce new requirements such as AI literacy, prompt engineering, critical evaluation of AI outputs, and responsible AI use.

Although many studies have investigated AI applications in Requirements Engineering and software development, few focus specifically on the competencies Business Analysts need in AI-supported environments. Existing studies either examine AI technologies or analyze general labor market trends, leaving limited evidence that connects academic findings with industry expectations for Business Analyst roles.

This study addresses this gap by reviewing recent literature on AI in Requirements Engineering and Business Analysis, while comparing academic findings with current Business Analyst job market expectations.

---

## 5. Motivation

The rapid adoption of AI is reshaping software development practices and changing the responsibilities of Business Analysts.

Recent literature demonstrates that AI can support many Requirements Engineering activities, but human expertise remains essential for stakeholder communication, strategic decision-making, requirements validation, and business understanding. At the same time, industry increasingly expects Business Analysts to possess AI-related competencies alongside traditional business analysis skills.

However, the relationship between AI-supported Requirements Engineering research and industry skill requirements remains insufficiently synthesized. A literature review can help identify emerging competencies, highlight consistent findings across studies, and reveal gaps between academic recommendations and industry expectations.

The findings of this study may support students, educators, researchers, and practitioners in understanding how the Business Analyst role is evolving in the AI era.

---

## 6. Target Context

**Professionals:** Business Analysts, Requirements Engineers, Systems Analysts, Product Managers, and software professionals working with AI-supported development processes.

**Organizations:** Software development companies adopting AI tools within Requirements Engineering and Business Analysis activities.

**Geography:** International literature and global industry evidence.

**Education:** Universities and professional training programs preparing future Business Analysts.

**Literature Scope:** Recent academic studies focusing on AI, Large Language Models, Requirements Engineering, Business Analysis, and software development.

---

## 7. Research Questions

**RQ1.** What AI-related skills are identified in the existing literature as important for Business Analysts working in software development environments?

**RQ2.** How do current studies describe the impact of AI on Business Analyst responsibilities and Requirements Engineering activities?

**RQ3.** What research gaps remain regarding AI competencies required for Business Analysts in software development?
